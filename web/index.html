Impedance analysis in three steps. Each step refines the one before; the colours on the board show the latest result.

  1. Board, stackup and targets
    Runs on loading: layers and materials, net classes and their target impedances.
  2. Impedance estimate
    Closed-form formulas (Hammerstad-Jensen, Wheeler, Wadell, IPC-2141) for every track, about a second. Typically within 5 % for single lines, 5–15 % off for coupled and coplanar lines.
  3. Field solver
    2D field solver (js_2d_fields) for each distinct cross-section: within 2 % of HFSS, seconds to minutes. Results are kept in this browser.

Accuracy

Impedance. Formulas (Hammerstad-Jensen, Wheeler, Wadell, IPC-2141): microstrip within 1–2 %, coupled and coplanar lines 5–15 % off (asymmetric stripline +11 %). Field solver: js_2d_fields by Henrik Forstén (GPL-3.0), quasi-static, included unmodified (revision ): within 1.7 % of the HFSS/Ansys references in his test suite, εeff within 1 %.

Lines and vias. RLGC within 2 % of HFSS; copper roughness presets are estimates. Vias as a physics-based ladder (return vias, antipads, stubs, back-drilling, pairs), compared with 3D results of kisi's FDTD solver: via impedance in the TDR median 1.4 %, 90th percentile 7.4 %; pairs within 3.5° phase; stub notch within 3 %.

Channel. Parasitics of series parts and packages are estimates (data/components.json). The eye uses the profile's linear transmitter and receiver: no IBIS models, equalisation, jitter or noise. The channel behind a connector is a simple lossy line.

Still open: validation against other solvers and against measurements (a test board is planned). Treat the numbers as plausible estimates, not as verified results.
